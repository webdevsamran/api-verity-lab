# Evidence packs for SOC 2, ISO 42001, DORA and the EU AI Act

```bash
apiverity evidence run-*.json -o evidence/
apiverity verify evidence/
```

## What an auditor is actually asking for

Not a tool's opinion about compliance. Records: what was checked, when, against
what, by what, and what came back — in a form that cannot have been edited
afterwards without anyone noticing.

`apiverity evidence` assembles exactly that from result artifacts this tool
already writes. It copies each one in, renders the
[OWASP mappings](compliance-mapping.md) beside it, writes an index naming what
each record demonstrates, and checksums the lot into a `SHA256SUMS` that
`apiverity verify` reads.

## The line this will not cross

It does not score, certify, or say a control is satisfied.

Mapping a technical record to a clause of a regulation is a judgement about an
organisation, not about a file. A tool that printed `CC8: PASS` would be
inventing an assessment nobody performed, and an auditor who found one would be
right to distrust everything else in the pack.

So the pack names **practices** and, for each, where that practice is usually
evidenced in each regime — with the citation and a note on what the record does
*not* establish:

| Practice | Evidenced by |
|---|---|
| Change control over a published interface | `breaking`, `diff`, `mcp-lock`, `changelog` |
| Verification that a running system still matches what it declared | `drift`, `test`, `coverage` |
| Inventory of the tools and servers an automated agent can reach | `mcp-inventory`, `mcp-lock`, `drift` |
| Detection of credential exposure and tool-description tampering | `validate`, `drift` |
| A dated, tamper-evident record of each verification | the pack itself |
| A record of who changed a policy, granted an approval, or enrolled a worker | [`audit export`](audit-export.md), verifiable away from the server |
| A kill-switch procedure, and evidence it was exercised | [`freeze`](kill-switch.md), with both transitions in the audit chain |

A practice with no artifact behind it is left out rather than listed as
unevidenced. A pack is a record of what was done; an empty row invites the
reader to imagine it was done badly rather than not at all.

## Citations, and one that is deliberately missing

References were read on 2026-09-09 and each one carries its source:

- **SOC 2** — Trust Services Criteria, common criteria CC1–CC9. Change control
  lands at CC8, monitoring at CC4, operations at CC7, access at CC6.
- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023** — Annex A groups A.5 (AI system lifecycle), A.7
  (system information), A.9 (third-party relationships).
- **DORA** (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) — Article 9 protection and prevention,
  Article 10 detection, Chapter V (Articles 28–30) ICT third-party risk.
- **EU AI Act** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) — Article 14 human oversight,
  Article 15 accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity, and Articles 12 and 19 on
  record-keeping.

ISO 42001's Annex A group **A.10 is never cited**, anywhere, because its title
could not be established from a source this project could read. An invented
citation is worse than a missing one, and in a pack an auditor is reading, far
worse.

The entry for Articles 12 and 19 says in the pack itself that **this does not
satisfy either of them**. Those articles require logs generated by the
high-risk AI system over its lifetime and retained by the deployer. An evidence
pack is a log of governance activity performed *on* an interface, which is a
different artifact, offered as supporting evidence only.

## Why it is not reproducible

Every other artifact this tool writes is deterministic. This one carries the
date it was made, because an evidence record with no date is not evidence.
`--as-of` fixes the timestamp to rebuild a historical pack, and with it fixed,
two runs over the same inputs produce identical checksums.

## Why the pack is flat

`apiverity verify` walks the files in a directory, not its subdirectories. A
`controls/` folder would sit unverified inside a pack that reported itself
verified — the exact class of defect `verify` was written to fix — so the
rendered mappings are prefixed filenames alongside the records instead.
